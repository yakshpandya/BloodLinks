After You Donate Blood

Discover the journey your blood takes after it leaves your arm.

What Happens to Your Blood?

Your donated blood goes through a rigorous process before it reaches a patient in need. Here's the incredible journey of a single unit of blood — from your arm to saving a life.

The Journey of Donated Blood

1

Collection & Labelling

Your blood is collected in a sterile bag and immediately labelled with a unique barcode. The bag is kept in a temperature-controlled environment to maintain safety.

2

Testing

Every unit is tested for infectious diseases including HIV, Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C, Malaria, and Syphilis using advanced techniques to ensure safety for the recipient.

3

Component Separation

Most donated blood is separated into its components: Red Blood Cells (stored up to 42 days), Platelets (5 days), and Fresh Frozen Plasma (1 year). Each component helps a different patient.

4

Storage

Components are stored in blood banks at precisely controlled temperatures until they're needed. A robust inventory management system tracks every unit.

5

Transfusion

When a patient needs blood, the right component is cross-matched with their blood type and transfused. Your one donation can help up to 3 different patients.

Who Needs Your Blood?

  • Cancer patients undergoing chemotherapy
  • Accident and trauma victims
  • Mothers with childbirth complications
  • Patients undergoing major surgeries
  • Children with severe anaemia or sickle cell disease
  • Patients with blood clotting disorders

Every 2 seconds, someone in India needs blood. There are currently no artificial substitutes for human blood — only your voluntary donation can save these patients.
